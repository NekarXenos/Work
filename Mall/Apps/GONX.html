Before you play

  • Nothing here reaches out without you asking. Your camera and location are never touched, and nothing you play or record leaves this device. The microphone is used in one place only — the Record button on the Sampler screen — and only once you press it and allow the prompt. If a permission prompt appears at any other moment, it isn't coming from here — decline it.
  • The Sampler holds sixteen slots of your own audio. Load files, resample a take you just played, or record straight off the microphone, then trim, tune and play them from the keys, the neck or the pads. Slots are kept in this browser's own storage so the rack is still there next time; Clear every slot on that screen removes them.
  • Audio needs one tap to start. Browsers block sound until you interact with the page, which is what the button above is for.
  • On a phone, Keys and Sound are separate screens. The keyboard fills the screen so nothing is cut off, and the tabs at the top switch between playing and editing the sound.
  • Instrument layout is behind the ♫ button in the play bar. Guitar, bass and the neck settings open in a sheet over the keys, so the playing surface keeps the whole screen.
  • Drum pads carry the shape of the part they fire. Choosing them switches to the built-in kit, which needs no connection. If your screen reports how hard you touched it, that sets the volume — on the pads, the keys and the strings alike.
  • Guitar and bass split in half when the phone is upright. Fret chord shapes on the bottom board, then strum across the strings on the top board to sound them — or turn on "Silence Chords" so only the strum sounds. Turning on "Whammy" lets the top board bend pitch by where you press it (closer to the neck edge dips it, closer to the bridge edge raises it); it's off by default since the constant retuning can lag on slower phones. Easy Chords also works on the neck now: one finger plays a major chord, add a minor 3rd for minor, or fret two frets apart on one string for a 7th.
  • SoundFont voices need a connection. The built-in oscillator voices work offline; the MIDI SoundFont presets download from a CDN.
  • Sing turns a tune in your head into a .mid file. Hum or whistle a line — one note at a time, not a chord — and the Sing screen writes it out as notes you can then hand any instrument and any tempo. If the microphone is not available on your phone, load a voice recording from the file picker instead; everything after that works the same.

If a swipe across the keys cuts out

  • Use fullscreen, or add the page to your home screen. Android gesture navigation claims swipes that begin near the screen edge and cancels them mid-glissando.
  • Start the swipe away from the left and right edges of the screen.
  • Turn off any browser "swipe to go back" or edge-gesture setting.

How it fits the rest of the synth
Pick Sampler at the top of the Sound screen and the keys, the neck and the drum pads all play these slots instead of a synth voice. On the drum-pad surface the sixteen slots become a four-by-four grid.

A slot is a real voice like any other: it runs through distortion, wah, EQ and echo, so Save .wav captures it, and Glide and string bends retune it by transposing the sample, the way a tracker does.

Slots live in this browser's storage, not on a server. A long file is cut to 30 seconds when it loads, which is what keeps a rack of sixteen a sane size to keep and to carry.

If it heard you wrong
These change how the recording is read, so they take a fresh pass over the audio — the roll redraws a moment after you let go.
Higher picks up softer singing and breath tails, and also more of the room. Lower ignores everything but the notes you leaned into.
Anything briefer is thrown away as a slip or a breath. Raise it if the roll is speckled with tiny notes; lower it for something fast.
How far your voice has to move before it counts as a different note rather than a scoop or vibrato. Raise it if a wobbly held note came out as a stutter of neighbours.
Two “la la” on the same pitch are one long note to a pitch detector; the attack is the only thing that tells them apart.
What it can and cannot hear
This follows one voice at a time. A hum, a whistle, a sung vowel, a solo instrument — anything with a single clear pitch — transcribes well. Chords, two people at once, and drums have no single pitch to find, so they come out as nonsense. Held vowels beat words: consonants are noise, and noise is where a detector goes wrong.

Everything runs in this browser. The recording is never uploaded, and closing the tab is the end of it — the .mid file you save is the thing that lasts, so save before you go.

A recording longer than 60 seconds is cut to 60. Sing a phrase at a time and stitch them together in whatever you open the files in; it also gives the detector an easier job than a whole song does.
