Seams

Points snap to a panel's corner (a node) first, then onto its edge: a seam, or the open border of the surface. A seam drawn from edge to edge splits its panel: when both ends land on a seam or the border, Enter cuts it as one seam. Start on the loose end of a crease to take the whole crease into your seam; Enter then closes the panel at its other end. Drag a node to move it and its seams.

Afterwards, tap any seam to remove it (Remove seam mode).

A floating seam stops inside a panel instead of closing or running from edge to edge, so it splits nothing. Untick to keep slits cut with Cut open.

Proposes seams that cut panels down to the vinyl: a panel wider than the roll is split along its length, one that wraps round the body where it turns, and one that would leave more waste than allowed in its rectangle (a frame, an L) at its inner corners. Bleed from Export is kept round every piece. Drag the end of a suggestion onto another corner to move it.

The underside is checked for a crease boundary when a model loads.

Creases

A crease is a line kept sharp without cutting the panel apart: a character line or a fold. Retopology keeps every crease where it is. Draw one in Crease mode as you would a seam; Enter finishes it, and Mirror seams mirrors it.

A floating seam splits nothing; as a crease it holds its line instead. In Crease mode, tap one to turn just that one.

Marks the lines of edges sharper than the crease angle in Seams, all but those shorter than a polygon. Remove seam mode takes a crease away too.

Mirrored pairs—

Touch the mirror plane to draw one symmetrical centre panel. If you started away from the plane, the next point joins your first point so you can draw the other edge. End on the plane, or press Enter to extend the unfinished edge toward its reflection and finish at the plane. A cyan marker shows when a point snaps. Mirrored twins share one colour; the Atlas section decides whether they also share an island.

Panels

Use Wrap panels to click panels on the model, or tick them below. Only checked panels are unwrapped. Glass, plastic and the underside can stay in the model for game vehicles.

List selections frame the clicked panel. A dotted outline through the model shows its selected mirror. This toggle is independent of Mirror seams.

    Intakes

    Vinyl is laid flat over an intake, a vent or a grille, then heated and pressed into it. Find intakes rolls a ball as wide as the widest intake over the wrap panels: wherever it cannot touch is a negative space. Each one accepted gets a lid over its opening — a taut film, held along its edge and along any seam it runs to — and the artwork is projected straight down from the lid into it.

    Dents shallower than 5 mm, and gentle hollows the ball can roll into, are left as they are: the vinyl follows them. In Intakes mode, tap one the search missed to add it, or tap a projected one to take it away.

    An opening that goes right through is covered: the artwork runs across it and the cut line skips it. Trim it and tuck the edge on the car.

    Untick to compare with flattening the panels as they are, walls and all.

    Retopology

    Cutting seams leaves slivers of polygons along them, and imported models bring poles and flipped faces. They stretch when a panel is flattened. Once the seams are in, check the wrap panels and rebuild the ones that need it into even triangles before you unwrap.

    Left empty, rebuilt panels take the model’s own size.

    A rebuild keeps the seams, the creases, the open border and edges sharper than the crease angle in Seams. A quad is two triangles with the edge between them hidden, and exports as a quad. With Mirror seams on and every panel matched by its twin, one side is rebuilt and mirrored, so both sides get the same polygons. A panel next to a rebuilt one changes only in its row of triangles along their seam. Undo cut brings the old polygons back.

    Advanced

    A true hull stands well off the body over its broad hollows — between bonnet and windscreen, along a waist line — and artwork cast in from that far off is stretched where the hull slants. Relaxed, the hull is let down by a ball this wide: it still bridges every vent and gap the ball cannot enter.

    New shapes are copied across the mirror plane. Turning this off keeps what is already mirrored; only shapes drawn from then on stay on one side.
